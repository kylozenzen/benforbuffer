I’m also a builder — which makes me a better community leader.

I don’t just support creators and small businesses — I live their reality. I ship my own tools and games in public, gather feedback, and iterate when things don’t land.

Planet Strength (fitness app)

Progressive overload & accountability in a browser.

A browser-based fitness tracker designed to make progressive overload and streaks simple, not intimidating.

What it taught me:

  • • Onboarding friction kills more users than missing features.
  • • People need a single, clear “win” per session more than a dashboard of metrics.
  • • Consistent little nudges (streaks, micro-wins) matter more than occasional big pushes.

Signal (breathing & focus app)

Simple breathing, calm UI, real focus.

A minimal app for guided breathing and short focus sessions, built for people who live online.

What it taught me:

  • • Rituals beat reminders — people stick with simple, repeatable flows.
  • • Language and microcopy can either calm people or overwhelm them.
  • • Community stories (“this helped me get through…”) are fuel for product decisions.

Plot Twisted & other games

Trivia and game concepts tested with real players.

Humorous, technically-accurate-but-misleading movie and game quizzes built as browser experiences.

What they taught me:

  • • Delight and humor make people want to come back, not just click once.
  • • Clear difficulty tuning and feedback loops are crucial for keeping players in flow.
  • • Players love being part of the iteration process — they become co-creators, not just users.

All of this makes me more empathetic to Buffer’s customers — I know what it feels like to ship something, be proud of it, then watch where community either breathes life into it or quietly abandons it.

My Buffer community blueprint.

High-level by design, so it’s easy to scan. Each pillar could be deepened with the team — this is the foundation I’d bring on day one.

Discord

The always-on living room for creators.

  • • Welcome flows that tag creators vs. small businesses vs. social pros.
  • • Weekly rituals (Wins Monday, Workflow Wednesday, Open Studio Friday).
  • • Creator Circles: small, themed cohorts for deeper support.
  • • Private channels for power users and beta feedback.

Reddit

Honest conversations about workflows and experiments.

  • • Launch a subreddit centered on “show your system,” not “show your stats.”
  • • Weekly threads: workflows, creator hurdles, office hours.
  • • Transparent feature and roadmap conversations where it makes sense.
  • • Guardrails that keep it useful, not promotional.

Social engagement

Comment-first, human-first presence.

  • • Daily “community passes” through LinkedIn, Threads, X, Bluesky.
  • • Replies that spotlight community wins and stories, not just features.
  • • Turning high-signal threads into case studies and resources.
  • • Close collaboration with Advocacy and Marketing to align tone and timing.

Power users & insights

From “happy users” to high-signal collaborators.

  • • Identify power users across Discord, Community inbox, and social.
  • • Create a structured beta / advocates group with clear perks.
  • • Stand up a “Community Signals” digest for Product & Growth.
  • • Ship a light “state of the community” snapshot on a regular cadence.

How I’d spend my first 90 days.

Listen first, then pilot, then scale what actually works. No stunts — just sustainable community building.

Days 0–30 · Learn & map

Listen deeply, understand the current ecosystem, and earn trust.

  • • Audit Discord, Community inbox usage, social engagement, and prior programs.
  • • Meet with Marketing, Advocacy, Product, and Engineering partners.
  • • Run listening sessions with community members to hear what’s working and what’s not.
  • • Stand up a simple “Community Signals” doc to capture patterns and quick wins.

Days 31–60 · Pilot & structure

Introduce light structure and test small bets.

  • • Launch 1–2 Discord rituals and refine based on participation and sentiment.
  • • Soft-launch the subreddit with clear posting patterns and guidelines.
  • • Identify 10–20 power users across channels and invite them into a pilot feedback group.
  • • Work with cross-functional partners to integrate community feedback into product and content decisions.

Days 61–90 · Scale & share

Double down on what works and communicate clearly.

  • • Expand successful programs and clearly communicate their purpose to the community.
  • • Formalize the power user / beta program with defined feedback loops and recognition.
  • • Partner with Growth and Brand to weave community stories into launch moments.
  • • Publish a light “state of the community” snapshot with honest wins and learnings.
