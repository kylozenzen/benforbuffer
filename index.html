San Antonio Zoo · deeper context

At the zoo, volume wasn’t the problem — we already had millions of followers. The challenge was turning that crowd into community that showed up, donated, and came back.

  • Built recurring series around big moments (Zoo Boo, Zoo Lights, new exhibits) so they felt like traditions the community owned.
  • Collaborated with PR, education, and sponsorship to make sure campaigns tied to real goals: attendance, memberships, sponsor value, and conservation storytelling.
  • Used comments, DMs, and in-person conversations to inform content choices — the best posts came straight from guest questions and keeper stories.

Same lens I’d bring to Buffer: fewer one-off “announcements,” more recurring, community-owned rituals that support growth and retention.

If I joined Buffer…

What I’d focus on first.

A simple north star, a few focused bets, and clear feedback loops so community isn’t just “vibes” — it’s part of how Buffer grows.

Community north star

Build spaces where Buffer customers feel:

  • Seen — their use cases and constraints are reflected back.
  • Supported — they have people and resources to lean on.
  • Involved — their feedback clearly shapes the product and story.

Discord as “the studio”

  • Tighten onboarding so new members know where to go and who to meet.
  • Introduce 1–2 weekly rituals (wins, workflow show-and-tell) and measure participation + sentiment.
  • Highlight member stories, not just Buffer updates.

Reddit as “the workbench”

  • Launch a workflow-first subreddit around setups, experiments, and honest questions.
  • Use weekly threads and office-hour style posts for deeper, searchable discussions.
  • Keep it useful and candid — never just a repost of marketing content.

Social engagement layer

  • Comment-first strategy on LinkedIn, Threads, X, Bluesky.
  • Replies that showcase community wins and “you said / we did” moments.
  • Work with marketing to turn high-signal conversations into content and case studies.

Signals & advocates

  • Identify power users across Discord, Community, and social (beyond follower counts).
  • Create a lightweight beta / advocate group with clear expectations and perks.
  • Ship a simple “Community Signals” digest for Product, Advocacy, and Growth.

How I’m already thinking in “Buffer.”

I’ve spent time digging through Buffer’s blog, product updates, and community pages — not just to learn features, but to understand the why behind them.

  • Discord is positioned as a gentle accountability space and creator camp, not just another server. I’d keep that human tone while layering in clearer journeys for different types of members.
  • Product decisions like Threads support and the Community inbox clearly came from listening to thousands of users. I’d make sure Discord, Reddit, and social replies feed that loop in a more structured way.
  • Transparency is a core value — from open salaries to sharing “small improvements.” Community work should mirror that with regular “here’s what we heard, here’s what we tried, here’s what changed” updates.

My goal isn’t to wallpaper Buffer with “Ben ideas,” but to deepen what’s already working and keep a clear line from community → product → story.

First 90 days

How I’d ramp in, realistically.

Days 0–30 · Listen

  • Audit Discord, Community usage, and organic Reddit mentions.
  • Meet with Advocacy, Marketing, Product, and Engineering to understand goals and current experiments.
  • Run a few short listening calls with creators and small businesses.
  • Share a simple first “Community Signals” rundown with early patterns, not conclusions.

Days 31–60 · Pilot

  • Launch one Discord ritual and a focused initial Reddit presence.
  • Invite a small wave of power users into a beta / advocate pilot.
  • Align with marketing on where community best supports upcoming launches.
  • Publish “what we tried / what we learned” updates internally.

Days 61–90 · Systemize

  • Double down on rituals that work; gracefully sunset anything that doesn’t land.
  • Formalize a lightweight but delightful advocate program.
  • Integrate community stories into launch playbooks and content calendars.
  • Share a “state of the community” snapshot with honest wins and growth edges.
